#11 From Steps to Curves: Simulating Brownian Motion

In our previous simulations, we explored:

  • Random Walk: Discrete Time, Discrete State (Steps of $\pm 1$).
  • Poisson Process: Continuous Time, Discrete State (Counting jumps of $+1$).

Now we complete the picture with the Brownian Motion (or Wiener Process). This is a stochastic process with both continuous time and continuous state. It describes the random motion of particles suspended in a fluid, or the theoretical movement of stock market prices.

1. Constructing the Process

Just as we approximated the Poisson process with small Bernoulli trials, we approximate Brownian motion by taking $n$ small time steps of size $dt = T/n$.

Instead of a jump of $\pm 1$ or $0/1$, the change in value (the "jump") $dW$ over a tiny time interval $dt$ is a random variable drawn from a Normal Distribution: $$ dW_t \sim N(0, dt) = \sqrt{dt} \cdot Z \quad \text{where } Z \sim N(0, 1) $$

The recurrence relation for the simulation is: $$ W_{t+dt} = W_t + \sigma \sqrt{dt} Z_t $$ Where $\sigma$ is the diffusion coefficient (volatility).

2. Generating Normal Jumps: The Box-Muller Transform

Computers typically generate Uniform(0, 1) random numbers. To simulate Brownian motion, we need to transform these uniform numbers into Standard Normal $N(0, 1)$ numbers. We use the Box-Muller Transform.

Given two independent uniform variables $u_1, u_2 \sim U(0, 1)$, we can generate two independent standard normal variables $z_1, z_2$: $$ z_1 = \sqrt{-2 \ln u_1} \cos(2\pi u_2) $$ $$ z_2 = \sqrt{-2 \ln u_1} \sin(2\pi u_2) $$

3. Theoretical Properties

  • Normality: The value $W(t)$ at any time $t$ follows a Normal distribution $N(0, t)$.
  • Continuous Paths: Unlike the Poisson process, Brownian paths are continuous everywhere (no jumps), but nowhere differentiable (extremely "jagged").
  • Self-Similarity: If you zoom in on the graph, it looks statistically identical to the zoomed-out version (fractal property).
